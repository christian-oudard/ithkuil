## Translation Techniques (from community discussions)

### 1. Making Verbs vs. Nouns

Stress determines word class:
- **Penultimate stress** (default) = noun/stative
- **Ultimate stress** (-á, -éu, etc.) = verb/dynamic

> "you can just shift the stress to penultimate position so as to make the
> word a noun instead of a verb" — ntsekees

### 2. Expressing "to be"

There is no verb "to be" in Ithkuil. Instead:
- Use the content word itself as the verb
- Apply appropriate specification (BSC, CTE, CSV, OBJ)

> "I've met the difficult sentence 'Are you dangerous animals?'. So I need
> a verb to put the VER illocution on it. But there's no verb 'to be' in
> Ithkuil." — vlad_utyumov

Solution: make "animal" the verb and apply VER illocution.

### 3. Case Frames & Modals

Modal constructions require special handling:
- The verb in a modal takes RPV (reportive) essence when not a real scenario
- The subject of a modal takes activative (ACT) case

> "modals take activative case as a subject, and the verb in a modal
> construction is usually RPV when they aren't real scenarios yet"
> — persimmonfans

### 4. Expressing Possession

Possessed nouns take stimulus (STM) case:

> "Uřkwájao (entitlements)-STM. STM cuz JQ says that's what possessed
> nouns should be in" — kosmic_dreamer_the_rainnightwing

### 5. The Carrier Root

Used for proper names and foreign words:
- Place it immediately before the name: `sala Säm` (carrier + "Sam")
- The carrier end adjunct marks boundaries when multiple names appear

> "it should be immediately after the carrier root, e.g. *sala Säm*"
> — persimmonfans

### 6. Using Specification Effectively

The four specifications (BSC, CTE, CSV, OBJ) let you choose what aspect
of a concept you're discussing:

> "Let's take the word for 'give' (enal). In Ithkuil, the receiver takes
> dative (ü), the giver may take inducive (u), and the gift takes
> thematic (a or no vowel)." — no.name_0101

| Specification | What it picks out |
|---|---|
| BSC (basic) | The holistic concept |
| CTE (content) | The essential quality/experience |
| CSV (constitutive) | The physical/tangible form |
| OBJ (objective) | The purpose/function/use |

### 7. Concatenation for Compound Meanings

Type 1 (hyphenated) and Type 2 (`a'` prefix) concatenation let you
combine formatives:

> "If you really want to make the relationship explicit maybe
> concatenation could do the trick? Like You-STM-Love I-ATT"
> — kosmic_dreamer_the_rainnightwing

### 8. Ca Stacking

When the Ca complex gets too complicated, use Ca stacking affixes:

> "If the CA-complex is too complicated, maybe we can use CA-stacking to
> reduce its complexity, especially to avoid gemination"
> — lijunwei_

### 9. The PVS Referential

The provisional (PVS) referential serves as a placeholder for unknowns
and wh-questions:

> "The PVS referential is there as a 'placeholder' of sorts to which we
> can assign a case. It's particularly useful for shorthand in-context
> wh-questions like 'I want a toy' [points at toy isle] 'which one?' (PVS)"
> — kosmic_dreamer_the_rainnightwing

### 10. "If-Then" Constructions

Don't use the conjectural for subordinate clauses:

> "that shouldn't be the default way to express 'if-then', because the
> conjectural cannot work in subordinate clauses (i.e. case frames)"
> — ntsekees

Use the DPC (Dependent Circumstances) affix instead.

## Common Corrections & Pitfalls

### 1. Forgetting to Make a Word a Verb
Many learners write all nouns when at least one word should have
ultimate stress to serve as the verb.

> "Also you haven't made any word a verb. I'd give the first one
> ultimate stress." — no.name_0101

### 2. Wrong Case on Referentials
ERG (li) vs THM (lu) vs ABS (la) choice matters:

> "lo should be THM, not ERG" — nvalce (on a non-verb referential)

### 3. Elision Rules
Slot IV default -a- can be elided, but non-default values cannot:

> "elision can't occur here because slot IV is -ä- instead of
> default -a-" — klarijon

### 4. Frame-Ending Affixes
TPF (temporal frame) affixes shouldn't be on the framed verb itself:

> "frame-ending TPF affixes shouldn't be on a framed verb itself. In
> this case you'd probably need to stack the TPF on a separate word."
> — persimmonfans

### 5. Ca Gemination Ordering
When Slot V affixes are present, Ca gemination rules (section 3.6.1)
apply, but rule 9 is notoriously ambiguous:

> "rule 9 seems either too arbitrary letting the user choose if something
> sounds 'euphonically awkward', or too complex requiring the user to
> remember all the phonotactics on the fly" — sejden
